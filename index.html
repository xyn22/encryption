1 · Computing powers mod n

RSA is one operation: me mod n, with exponents hundreds of digits long. This lesson is how to compute it without ever touching a big number.

  • You may reduce mod n after every multiplication. The answer does not change.
  • One at a time: each step multiplies by a, so the exponent goes up by 1.
  • Square and multiply: split the exponent into powers of 2, build each row by squaring the row above (the exponent doubles), then multiply the rows you picked (exponents add).

2 · Loops and tails

Powers mod n always end up cycling. That cycle is what lets RSA bring m back.

  • Each value is the previous one times a, mod n. Only n remainders exist, so the walk must repeat.
  • If the walk reaches 1, it restarts from the beginning. Exponents then only matter mod the loop length.
  • Exponents of the form (multiple of the loop) + 1 bring you back where you started.
  • A tail (values never revisited) needs a factor shared between a and n.
